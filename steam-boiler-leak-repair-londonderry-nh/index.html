Steam Boiler Leak Repair: A Londonderry, NH Homeowner Guide

Where steam boilers leak, what is safe to do before help arrives, and what a repair visit looks like.

By the Londonderry Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

Call (603) 815-9179

A steam boiler leak is usually one of three things: a worn fitting or valve, a failing return pipe, or a crack in the boiler itself. Puddles under the boiler, a steady drip at the gauge glass, or a feeder that never stops running all point to a leak somewhere in the loop. The first move is to switch the boiler off at its emergency switch and keep everyone clear of hot parts.

Do not open the relief valve to see what happens, and do not refill a boiler that has gone dry while it is still hot. Adding cold water to a hot, empty boiler can crack it. If you smell gas or oil fumes, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

This guide walks through the common leak points, a safe homeowner checklist, and the repair options. For active leaks on a heating loop, our 24/7 line at (603) 815-9179 can send a licensed plumber, and our leak detection team can trace the wet spot when the source is hidden.

How a steam system loses water

A steam boiler heats water until it turns to steam. The steam rises through supply pipes to the radiators, gives up its heat, turns back to water and runs down the return pipes to the boiler. Every part of that loop can leak.

Because the system loses a little water as steam vents out, the boiler has an automatic feeder or a manual fill valve to make it up. A feeder that runs often is a warning sign. It may be hiding a leak that you never see, because the new water keeps the level normal.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Take these steps in order. They keep people safe and limit the damage.

  • Turn off the boiler at the emergency switch, usually on the wall near the stairs or at the top of the basement steps.
  • Close the water feed to the boiler if there is a shutoff valve on the feeder line.
  • Move stored boxes and anything electrical away from the wet area.
  • Do not touch hot pipes, the gauge glass or the relief valve discharge.
  • Take a photo of the wet area and the pressure gauge for the plumber.
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide detector near the boiler room and bedrooms.

If the leak is a thin spray or steam from a pipe, stay away and call at once. Steam burns fast. The 24/7 line is staffed at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

Why the water level matters

The sight glass on the side of a steam boiler shows the water level. It should sit near the middle. A level that keeps dropping means water is leaving the system faster than the feeder replaces it.

Low water is dangerous. A boiler that fires with too little water can overheat, and the low water cutoff exists to stop it. If the cutoff is clogged with sediment, it may fail to shut the burner off. A plumber checks and flushes the cutoff as part of most boiler visits.

Residential steam boilers normally run at very low pressure, usually under 3 psi. If the gauge climbs well above that, shut the boiler off and call. High pressure points to a stuck pressurestat, a clogged pigtail or another control problem that has nothing to do with the leak but will make it worse.

Repair or replace? An honest comparison

Most steam leaks are cheap fixes at fittings, valves and gauge parts. A leak in the cast iron boiler body is a different matter. Sections can be sealed in some cases, but a boiler that has leaked at the seams often keeps leaking, and repeated repairs add up.

Repair makes sense when

The leak is at a valve, gauge, feeder or a short pipe section, the boiler is otherwise heating evenly and the rest of the piping is sound.

Replacement deserves a look when

Water is weeping from the boiler body, the same section has been patched before, or the system needs repeated feeder runs to keep its level.

A radiator or pipe replacement fits when

One radiator or a stretch of return line is corroded and the rest of the system is in good shape.

A second opinion helps when

Someone quotes a full boiler replacement without showing you where the water is escaping. Ask to see the leak.

Boiler replacement also touches gas, oil, venting and electrical work, so ask any contractor how those trades will be handled and who pulls the permit. For related heating-water plumbing, see our plumbing installation page.

What a steam boiler leak repair visit includes

  1. Safety checkThe plumber shuts down the boiler, checks the water level and pressure, and looks for fuel or gas odors before touching anything.
  2. Find the sourceWater is traced from the wettest point back to the leak. Dye, a flashlight and a dry paper towel on each joint go a long way.
  3. Test the controlsThe feeder, low water cutoff and pressurestat are tested to learn whether a control problem caused the leak.
  4. Repair and refillThe failed part is replaced, the boiler is refilled slowly and brought up to temperature, and the joint is watched as it heats.
  5. Explain the findingYou get a plain explanation of what failed, what else is aging and what to watch over the winter.

What boiler leak repair costs

Boiler leaks vary from a valve packing to a failed section, and each is priced first. The number follows:

  • The part that failed. A gauge glass set and a boiler section are very different jobs.
  • How hard the leak is to reach, especially behind jacket panels or in a tight utility room.
  • Whether controls such as the feeder or low water cutoff also need replacing.
  • Whether the boiler has to be drained, flushed and refilled.
  • After-hours or holiday dispatch.

Londonderry, NH note: permits and licenses

A boiler replacement may need a permit. The town lists plumbing permits on its online portal and as a paper form, and Building and Zoning Enforcement can be reached at 603-432-1100, extension 440.

New Hampshire licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Board for the Licensing and Regulation of Plumbers. Ask any contractor for their license, and confirm it with the board at 603-271-3267. That call takes a few minutes and tells you who is allowed to touch your heating water piping.

Boiler piping and relief valves are code items, and plumbing connections follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
