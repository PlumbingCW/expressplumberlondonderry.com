How an emergency call from a neighboring town works

The process is the same in every town. You call (603) 815-9179, give the address, and describe what is happening. The dispatcher tells you what to shut off, and a licensed plumber is scheduled, with same-day appointments when available.

The town page for your area lists local details such as water source, ZIP codes and the municipal office to call for permits. Each page also explains the steps to take before the plumber arrives, because the first ten minutes often decide how much damage a leak does.

What to have ready when you call from a neighboring town

A complete address with the ZIP code lets the dispatcher match your home to the right town page and the right local details. Mention a cross street if your road shares a name with another one nearby, which is common across Rockingham and Hillsborough counties.

  • Whether your water comes from a private well or a public utility.
  • Whether water is still running, and where the main shutoff valve is.
  • Which fixtures are affected, and whether any sewage is involved.
  • A phone number the plumber can reach you on while driving in.

We do not promise an exact arrival time from any town, because traffic, weather and the length of the previous job all change it. The dispatcher gives you an honest estimate and tells you what to do while you wait.

What changes from town to town

Water source and sewer service differ across these communities. Windham homes mostly rely on private wells, Derry and Hudson run public water departments, and Litchfield has dealt with well contamination and water line extensions. A plumber who knows which setup you have diagnoses faster, so tell the dispatcher what you know.

If you are on a private well anywhere in this area, test your water and ask the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services about testing and treatment programs. For public water, ask your utility for its annual water quality report.
