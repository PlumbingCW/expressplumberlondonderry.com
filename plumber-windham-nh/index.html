Wells first: what Windham's water supply means for plumbing

The Windham town website calls wells the town's primary water supply and says 2,412 wells have been built since 1984. It puts the average bedrock well depth at 509 feet, which it describes as 34 percent deeper than the average New Hampshire bedrock well.

A deep well means a long run of pipe and a pump that works hard, and a failing pump looks a lot like a plumbing leak. If the water stops at every tap at once, check the breaker for the pump and call a well contractor. If one fixture is weak or the pressure swings after you shut off a leaking valve, call us.

The town page also says one out of five wells in New Hampshire and Windham has some level of arsenic. That is a water quality issue and not a plumbing repair, but it matters when you replace fixtures or add treatment. Test your tap water, and ask the state or a certified lab how to read the result.

Leaks that stay hidden until the bill or the stain shows up

On a well, a slow leak does not raise a utility bill, so it shows up as a pump that cycles more often or a stain that grows. A leak behind drywall can run for weeks before anyone notices. Our leak detection service uses listening gear and moisture meters to find the pipe without opening walls blindly.

Water heaters fail the same quiet way. A tank that weeps at the base or a relief valve that drips is a warning. We handle water heater repair on the spot when parts allow, and water heater replacement when the tank itself has failed.

Ask for a clear price before work starts. If the fix touches a well pump or pressure tank, we tell you that is outside what we do and point you to the right trade.

Before we arrive in Windham: active leak checklist

On a well, the pump keeps pushing water into a broken pipe, so stopping the supply matters even more.

Stop the supply

Close the main valve where water enters the house. Then switch off the well pump breaker so the pump does not keep feeding the leak.

Power down wet zones

Turn off breakers for any wet basement circuit, and the electric water heater if it is leaking. Stay out of standing water near outlets.

Clear and contain

Move stored items off the floor, and use towels or a wet vacuum on shallow water. Save any large flood for the plumber.
