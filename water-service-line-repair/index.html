Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Londonderry, NH

The water line from the street or the well to your house can leak for months before it shows.

A water service line leak is urgent when water is surfacing in the yard, when pressure drops suddenly, or when the meter keeps running with every fixture off. Our 24/7 line takes those calls at night and on weekends, and a licensed plumber can come the same day when the schedule allows.

While you wait, close the main valve inside the house. If the yard is flooding, note where the water appears and keep children away from soft ground. Do not dig near utility lines.

Meter still running? High water bill?

Many calls start with a symptom and not a diagnosis. The bill climbed. The meter spins when nothing is on. A wet patch appears where the grass is greener. Each of these can point to a leak in the service line.

  1. Turn everything offStop all taps, appliances and irrigation.
  2. Read the meterNote the leak indicator or low-flow dial, wait 15 minutes and read it again.
  3. Close the mainIf the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps running, the leak is on the service line side.
  4. Call usTell the dispatcher what you saw so we can bring the right tools.

Homes on private wells have no meter, so a pump that cycles constantly or a pressure tank that never holds is the clue.

A broken service line is an emergency: how we find the leak

When the line breaks, water can surface in the yard or flood a basement wall within minutes. Close the main shutoff, then call the 24/7 line so a plumber can be scheduled the same day when available.

A buried line can leak without any visible sign. We use pressure tests, acoustic listening gear and line tracers to find the spot. For plastic pipe, tracing needs a tone signal. Once the point is marked, we dig only where needed. Our leak detection page explains these tools in more detail.

Irrigation lines and meter-box fittings can also leak. We check them before assuming the main line has failed.

We trace the cause before we replace anything. A pressure reducing valve that has failed can push pressure up and burst fixtures, so we test it.

Repair or replace the line?

A short leak in good pipe can be repaired with a new section and proper fittings. Old galvanized, lead or failing plastic lines are better replaced. We tell you which case you have after we uncover the pipe.

Trenchless methods may be possible depending on depth and obstacles, though not every line suits them. When the line runs under a driveway, the conversation takes longer and we cover the choices clearly.

Town water, private wells and water main work

Whether your line comes from a town system or a private well changes who owns which part of it. Ask your utility or Londonderry Public Works at 603-432-1100, extension 460, where the town's responsibility ends and yours begins.

Town news lists water main extension projects for 2026 on Old Nashua Road, Old Derry Road and South Road. If your street is affected, expect pressure changes and delays and tell us about them.

Many Londonderry homes use private wells, and wells in this region have shown PFAS contamination linked to the former Saint-Gobain plant in Merrimack. Test your well water, and ask the state environmental agency about testing and treatment programs. A line repair is a good moment to check your water.
