Water Heater Installation in Londonderry, NH

A planned installation lets you choose the right heater, not just the one that fits the hole.

Call (603) 815-9179

Water heater installation in Londonderry, NH covers new construction, remodels and swaps you schedule before the old tank fails. A scheduled job is quieter than an emergency. You choose the type, we check the vent, gas, power and drain, and the work is done in one visit.

If your current heater has already failed and the house is cold, see our water heater replacement page instead. This page is for planned work.

What we check before we install

  • Where the heater will sit, how much clearance it has and whether the floor can carry a full tank.
  • Venting for gas or propane models, including the chimney liner or a sealed vent run.
  • Electrical capacity for electric and heat pump units.
  • A drain or pan that will catch a leak before it reaches finished floor.
  • Water pressure, so a pressure reducing valve or expansion tank is added if needed.

These checks are the difference between a heater that runs for years and one that needs repair in months. We record what we find and tell you what needs to change.

Londonderry has new development alongside older homes, including the Woodmont Commons area. New builds benefit from a plumber who sets the heater location, drain and vent before the walls close. Remodels often move a heater, and that changes the vent and supply.

If your project includes more than the heater, our plumbing installation team can handle fixtures and supply lines in the same visit.

Private wells and your new heater

Many Londonderry homes use private wells. Sediment and minerals in well water wear out tanks and clog tankless heat exchangers. Test your water before you pick a heater, and ask a water treatment professional about filters. The state environmental agency offers programs for private well testing.

A sediment filter ahead of the heater and a yearly flush plan protect your investment.

Sizing a heater for your household

Size depends on how many people shower, how many bathrooms you have and whether the washer and dishwasher run at the same time. A tank that is too small runs out. A tank that is too large wastes fuel keeping extra water hot.

Tell us about your household and any large soaking tub. We match the first hour rating, the amount of hot water a tank delivers in its first hour, to your peak morning demand.

Care after installation

Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year, and flush a tank heater to clear sediment. Keep the area around the unit clear of boxes and chemicals. Know where the cold water shutoff is, and test it so it moves.

Write the install date on the tank. It gives you a clear start for the 8 to 12 year planning window most tank heaters follow.

Permits and code

Plumbing work should meet the plumbing code your town enforces. The Building and Zoning Enforcement office, at 603-432-1100, extension 440, can confirm what your swap needs. Plumbing permits are available through the town's online portal or as a PDF form.

What installation costs

Installation is priced before the old tank is drained. These items shape the price:

  • The heater type and capacity.
  • New vent, gas line or electrical work.
  • Expansion tank, pressure reducing valve and drain pan.
  • Difficult access, long runs or code upgrades.
  • Removal of an old unit, if there is one.

Installations are not usually an insurance matter. Ask your utility about rebates for efficient models. For your own quote, call (603) 815-9179.
