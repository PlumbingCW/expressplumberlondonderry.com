What counts as a plumbing emergency

An emergency plumber is the right call when water is moving where it should not, or when a fixture has stopped working in a way that harms the house. Waiting makes most of these problems more expensive. Our 24/7 line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays.

  • A pipe has burst or a ceiling is dripping, and you cannot stop the water.
  • Sewage or drain water is coming up through a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • A water heater is leaking from the tank or the relief valve will not stop running.
  • The water meter keeps spinning after every fixture is off.
  • There is no hot water and the house is cold enough to risk frozen pipes.

While you wait for the plumber, shut off the main water valve, turn off power to wet areas if you can do it safely, and move belongings away from the water. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call us.

Private wells and town water in Londonderry

Many Londonderry homes get their water from private wells. PFAS contamination tied to the former Saint-Gobain plant in nearby Merrimack affected wells in this area, and as of January 2026 the town was developing a plan to extend public water through special assessment districts. The state environmental agency, NHDES, runs programs to help well owners outside the Saint-Gobain boundary test and treat their water.

For plumbing, that matters in two ways. A well home has a pressure tank and pump that can cause low pressure or short cycling, and a home on a new town water connection needs its service line and shutoff checked. Our water service line repair page explains both situations. If you rely on a well, test your water and ask NHDES which programs apply to your address.

We cannot tell you the hardness of your water without a test. Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report, or test your tap, before you choose a softener or filter.

How to check a plumber's license in New Hampshire

New Hampshire licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Board for the Licensing and Regulation of Plumbers, created by RSA 329-A. Ask any plumber for a license before work begins, and confirm it with the board at 603-271-3267 or at 21 South Fruit Street, Suite 24, Concord.

Permits depend on the job, and the town's Building and Zoning Enforcement office (603-432-1100, extension 440) is where to check before a water heater swap or a new line. The office is at 268B Mammoth Road, and the town lists plumbing permits on its online portal and as a paper form.

Our work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as New Hampshire adopts it through the State Building Code, and the town building office enforces it.
