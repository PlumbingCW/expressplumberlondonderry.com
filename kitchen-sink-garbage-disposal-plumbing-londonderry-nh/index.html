Kitchen Sink Plumbing With a Garbage Disposal: A Londonderry, NH Homeowner Guide

A disposal changes how a kitchen drain is built, and it changes where clogs and leaks show up.

By the Londonderry Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

Call (603) 815-9179

A kitchen sink with a garbage disposal has more connections than a bathroom sink. The basin drains into the disposal, the disposal drains into a trap arm, and the dishwasher usually ties into the disposal or the same branch. When one part fails, the symptom often appears somewhere else.

This guide explains how the pieces fit, why kitchen drains clog differently, and what you can check safely. If water is already on the floor, turn off the disposal switch, keep hands out of the unit, and read the section on leaks first.

How the pieces connect

Water leaves a double-basin sink through two strainers. One goes straight to the trap and the other goes through the disposal, and a short tailpiece joins them before the trap arm runs into the wall. The dishwasher drain hose connects to a nipple on the side of the disposal.

That layout explains a common call. If the sink backs up into the dishwasher or the other basin, the blockage sits past the point where they join. The disposal is not the cause, even though everyone blames it first.

Disposal

Grinds food waste and sends it into the trap. The motor, seals and mounting ring wear out over time.

Trap and trap arm

Holds a water seal against sewer gas and catches debris. Kitchen grease collects here and in the pipe beyond.

Dishwasher hose

Rises to a high loop under the counter and ties into the disposal inlet. A blocked knockout plug stops it draining.

Branch line

Runs inside the wall to the main drain. Cooking grease builds up here and a cable is needed to clear it.

Why kitchen drains clog

Cooking fat is the main culprit. It leaves the sink as liquid, cools in the pipe and sticks to the wall. Food scraps catch on it, and over months a 1.5 inch drain narrows to a thin channel.

Coffee grounds, eggshells, pasta and rice also settle into the trap. A disposal chops these into smaller pieces, but it does not carry them through the pipe. Running cold water for 15 seconds after grinding helps, and hot water alone does not wash grease out of a long run.

If the sink drains slowly even after you clear the trap, the grease is farther along. Our drain cleaning service uses a cable with a cutting head, and hydro jetting scours the pipe wall when grease keeps returning.

Disposal problems you can check yourself

  1. The unit hums but does not spinSwitch it off. Insert the hex key from the unit into the bottom socket and work it back and forth to free a jam. Press the red reset button on the bottom.
  2. The unit is silentCheck the reset button, then the wall switch and the breaker. A tripped reset means the motor overheated or jammed.
  3. The sink drains slowly with the disposal runningTurn it off, clear the trap and the tailpiece, and test with a pot of water. Slow flow with a clean trap points to the branch line.
  4. A bad smell returnsClean the rubber splash guard under the flange with a brush. Food residue collects there and the smell comes back unless you scrub it.

Never put a hand into the disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object. Unplug the unit or switch off its breaker first if you must reach inside.

Leaks under a kitchen sink

A disposal leak shows up in three places. Water at the top ring means the flange seal or mounting is failing. Water at the side means the dishwasher hose clamp or the drain outlet gasket is loose. Water at the bottom means the housing is cracked, and the unit must be replaced.

Dry every part with a paper towel, run the sink with the disposal off and then on, and watch where the first drop appears. If the leak is at the housing, shut off the breaker and plan a replacement. Our garbage disposal repair and installation page covers that work.

Leaks at the faucet supply lines or the shutoff valves also soak the cabinet. If you cannot find where the water comes from, leak detection pinpoints it before the cabinet floor rots.

Londonderry, NH note: septic or town sewer

Some Londonderry properties connect to the town sewer and others use a septic tank. That matters for a kitchen disposal, because ground food waste adds solids to a septic tank, and tanks vary in size and pumping schedule.

We cannot say which system serves your street. The Londonderry Department of Public Works lists sewer among its services and can be reached at 603-432-1100, extension 460. Ask them, then ask your septic pumper how often to pump if you have a tank and a disposal.

If you live in a neighboring town, the same question applies in Hudson or Litchfield, and our dispatchers can help you sort out what to do next.
