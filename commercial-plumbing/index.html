When a restroom backs up or a pipe lets go after closing time, a business cannot wait for Monday.

Commercial plumbing covers the water, drain and fixture systems in offices, shops, restaurants, light industrial buildings and multi-unit properties. A leak or backup in a commercial building can close a business for the day, so our 24/7 line takes after-hours calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, with same-day dispatch when schedules allow.

If water is spreading, shut off the main valve and any water heater feeding the affected area, and keep staff and customers away from wet electrical equipment. Then call (603) 815-9179 and tell the dispatcher the building type, the problem and whether a landlord or property manager must authorize work.

We handle repair and installation work for small and mid-size commercial properties. Some jobs, such as large boiler plants, medical gas and fire sprinkler systems, need specialty contractors, and we tell you when that is the case.

After-hours emergencies a business cannot wait out

  • A restroom, kitchen or floor drain is overflowing into customer or work areas.
  • A supply pipe has burst or a fitting is spraying above a ceiling or equipment.
  • The water heater has failed or is leaking in a space that needs hot water to operate.
  • A grease line has backed up and the kitchen cannot run.
  • A tenant reports water coming through a ceiling from the unit above.

For multi-tenant buildings, tell us who holds the keys and who authorizes the repair. A clear chain of contact saves time at 2 a.m. when a plumber needs to enter an unoccupied suite.

Backflow testing

A backflow preventer stops used or contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply. Irrigation systems, fire lines and some commercial equipment use them, and they need periodic testing by a certified tester.

Which assemblies need testing, and how often, is set by your water utility and the town. Your water provider and the town's Building and Zoning Enforcement office (603-432-1100, extension 440) can tell you what applies to your property before you book. We schedule these as planned visits and not as emergencies.

Grease traps and kitchen drains

A grease trap catches fats and oils before they reach the sewer. When it fills, the kitchen drains slow, the trap smells and grease can carry into the building line, which is a common cause of a restaurant shutdown.

We clean grease traps on a schedule that matches the kitchen's volume and clear the lines when a trap has overflowed. A regular cleaning costs less than an emergency, and a clear record helps if a sewer authority asks about it.

Storm drains and parking lot drainage

Roof drains, catch basins and trench drains collect leaves, silt and trash. When they clog, water pools in a lot or runs toward a doorway. We clear them with cable and jet equipment and camera the line when the same basin keeps filling.

A storm drain that discharges to a town system or a wetland falls under rules that vary by site. Ask the town before you flush anything unusual into it.

An honest note on commercial leak detection

Leak surveys on large buildings are different from a house visit. A fire line, a multi-building loop or a long underground main can take hours to isolate, and the answer is sometimes that the leak is in a part of the system we do not service.

We are direct about what we can find and when a specialty contractor is the better fit. Describe the building and the symptom first, and we will tell you whether a survey is realistic before anyone drives out.

What commercial plumbing costs

Business calls are quoted before work starts, with after-hours labor stated plainly. Cost depends on:

  • The size and type of the system, such as a single restroom or a multi-tenant stack.
  • Access, including ceilings, shafts and after-hours building entry.
  • Whether specialized equipment such as a jetter or a camera is needed.
  • Parts availability for commercial fixtures and heaters.
  • Emergency dispatch outside business hours.

Commercial property policies vary widely. Ask your agent whether they cover sudden water damage and business interruption. For a quote on your building, call (603) 815-9179.

Commercial fixture counts, backflow protection and pipe sizing are set by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) in the State Building Code, and your building's use decides which sections apply.
