Hydro Jetting Pipe Cleaning in Londonderry, NH: What to Know

Hydro jetting scrubs a pipe clean with high-pressure water. It is powerful, and it is not right for every pipe.

By the Londonderry Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

Call (603) 815-9179

Hydro jetting sends water at very high pressure through a hose and a nozzle inside a drain or sewer pipe. The nozzle blasts grease, scale and roots off the pipe wall, and the water flushes the debris downstream. Unlike a cable that punches a hole through a clog, jetting cleans the full circle of the pipe.

That makes it the better tool for repeat clogs, grease buildup and root regrowth. It also makes it the wrong tool for a fragile or collapsed pipe. This guide explains how the process works, what a good contractor checks first, and the questions to ask. If you want the service itself, see our hydro jetting page.

Jetting versus snaking

A drain snake or cable is a rotating steel line with a cutting head. It is quick and works well on a single soft clog. It leaves a thin channel through the middle of the blockage, and the rest of the buildup stays on the pipe wall.

Jetting is slower to set up and removes far more material. If a drain slows again a few weeks after a cable visit, the line probably needs jetting. Our drain cleaning team starts with a cable for most single-fixture clogs and moves to jetting when the camera shows buildup.

What a jetting visit looks like

  1. Camera firstA drain camera shows the pipe material, the clog and any cracks, offsets or collapsed sections before water pressure is used.
  2. Access pointThe hose enters through a cleanout, a removed toilet or a vent. A cleanout makes the job faster and safer.
  3. Cut and flushThe nozzle pushes forward cutting the buildup, then pulls back flushing it toward the sewer or tank.
  4. Second camera passA second look confirms the pipe is clean and notes any defects the buildup was hiding.
  5. ReportYou get an explanation of what was found and whether the line needs repair, lining or just a maintenance schedule.

When to skip jetting

High-pressure water can damage a pipe that is already failing. Cast iron with thin walls, clay pipe with broken joints and any pipe with a collapse or a major offset are poor candidates. In these cases, jetting can blow out a weak section and flood a basement or yard.

That is why the camera comes first. If the pipe is not strong enough, a good contractor will tell you so and suggest a gentler cleaning, a spot repair or a sewer line repair plan instead.

Signs your line may be ready for jetting

  • A kitchen drain clears with a cable and slows again within weeks.
  • Several fixtures gurgle or back up at once.
  • A restaurant, laundry or home with heavy cooking has frequent grease clogs.
  • A camera shows roots, scale or a thick coating inside the pipe.
  • The main line has been cabled more than once in a year.

If any of these sound familiar, ask for a camera inspection first. Our sewer inspection service documents the pipe so you can decide with real information.

What hydro jetting costs

Jetting is priced before the hose goes in. What moves that price:

  • Pipe length and diameter, and how many access points exist.
  • How heavy the buildup is and how many passes are needed.
  • Camera inspection before and after.
  • Whether a cleanout must be installed first.
  • After-hours or emergency dispatch.

Londonderry, NH note: sewer or septic changes the plan

Some Londonderry properties connect to a town sewer main, and properties without one use a septic system. Not sure which one you have? Public Works answers sewer questions at 603-432-1100, extension 460.

This matters for jetting because the debris has to go somewhere. On a sewer connection it moves down the line. On a septic system, flushing a heavy load of grease and roots into the tank can overload it. Ask your plumber where the debris will end up, and whether the tank should be pumped first.

Cleanout access is part of drain layout under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which matters for jetting.
