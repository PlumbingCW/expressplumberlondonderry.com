New construction plumbing: rough-in, inspection, finish

Plumbing a new home or addition happens in stages. The rough-in comes first, before walls close. The finish comes after, when fixtures go in.

  1. LayoutWe read the plans, place drains, vents and supply lines, and flag anything that conflicts with framing.
  2. Rough-inWe run drain, vent and supply pipe through the framing and install tub and shower valves.
  3. Pressure and leak testsWe test the lines before insulation and drywall cover them.
  4. InspectionNew lines and moved fixtures may involve a permit and an inspection, which the town's Building and Zoning Enforcement office (603-432-1100, extension 440) can walk you through. We coordinate our work around the inspection.
  5. FinishWe set fixtures, connect appliances and test every drain and supply.

Large developments such as Woodmont Commons, the 600-acre mixed-use project in town, are planned and built by their own contractors. We handle individual homes, additions and remodels.

Planning before you call

A few details speed up an accurate quote. Gather them before you call, and we will ask for the rest.

  • Which fixtures you want, and whether you have bought them.
  • Where the new fixture sits compared with existing pipes.
  • Whether the room is above a basement, crawlspace or slab.
  • Town water or a private well, and town sewer or septic.
  • Your timeline, including any inspection date.

If the new fixture ends in a drain that has been slow, run it through our drain cleaning crew first. Installing a new sink on a partly blocked line only hides the problem.

Water supply and drain connections

New plumbing is only as good as the supply behind it. If your house is on a private well, the pressure tank and pump set the pressure at every fixture. If you are on town water, a regulator may limit it. We check pressure with a gauge at a hose spigot before we add load.

Drains must slope, vent and connect to a line that can carry the added flow. A basement bathroom often needs a sewage ejector pump if the drain sits below the main line. We explain whether your project needs one and how it works.
